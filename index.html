About

I am a PhD student in Biostatistics at NYU Grossman School of Medicine, advised by Iván Díaz. My research focuses on developing causal inference methods to evaluate the effects of treatment strategies on health outcomes, with an emphasis on semiparametric theory and machine learning. My work is motivated by applications in neurology, health policy, and clinical decision-making. I also work with Nicholas Illenberger on measurement error correction in longitudinal causal inference.

Before joining NYU, I earned an MS in Biostatistics at Boston University, advised by Charlene J. Ong, Shariq Mohammed, and Laura White. My research assistant work with Dr. Ong at Boston Medical Center and Massachusetts General Hospital focused on dynamic risk prediction for patients with large ischemic stroke using longitudinal clinical data and brain imaging. At the University of Pennsylvania, I was advised by Scott L. Diamond on biomedical research and worked with Ryan J. Urbanowicz on developing machine learning pipelines for clinical outcome prediction. I also worked at Pfizer in the Biometrics & Data Sciences groups on clinical trial design and data science methods for trial monitoring.
